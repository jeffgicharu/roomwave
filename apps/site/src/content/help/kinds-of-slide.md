## Multiple choice

Up to six answers. Choose whether people may pick one or several. Short answers appear as columns; longer ones as rows, so they stay readable.

*Use it for:* decisions, opinions, "where are you joining from?"

## Word cloud

People send one, two or three short answers each, up to three words. The most common grow in the middle. Capitals and stray punctuation do not split a word in two, and offensive words are refused.

*Use it for:* moods, expectations, "one word for this week".

## Rating scale

A scale from 1 to 5, 7 or 10, with your own labels at each end. The screen shows how the answers are spread and the average.

*Use it for:* confidence, satisfaction, "how clear was that?"

## Audience Q&A

People send questions and upvote other people's. You can approve each question before it shows on the screen, and mark questions as answered.

*Use it for:* panels, town halls, the end of a lecture.

## Quiz question

Two to four answers with one correct, and a timer of 10 to 60 seconds. See [Running a quiz](/help/running-a-quiz) for how scoring works.

## Content slide

A heading, some words and a picture from the built-in library. People's phones show the heading while it is on screen.
